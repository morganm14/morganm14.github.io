# The problem

## Who has it
- A senior business major at Xavier looking for a full-time, entry-level job in Cincinnati, Chicago, Charlotte, Detroit or Charleston
- Open to several job titles, not just one (e.g., finance, analyst, researcher)

## What it costs them
- 5–7 hours a week searching
- 150–300 listings sorted through to find a few worth applying to

## How they handle it today
- Search LinkedIn, Handshake, Indeed and Glassdoor separately
- Enter the same filters on each: entry level, full time, salary, in person, city, field

## Why that falls short
- The filters are too coarse: even with everything set, there are far too many results
- Jobs labeled "entry level" often aren't really entry level
- No way to describe what they actually want to do, or to search several job titles at once

## How they would know it is solved
- A search returns 20–25 jobs instead of 150–300
- Job searching takes 1–2 hours a week instead of 5–7
- The results match who they are and what they want to do, not just the basic filters
