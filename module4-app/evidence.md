# Evidence

## The problem is real
What I need to show:
- Job searching costs a senior 5–7 hours a week
- They sort through 150–300 listings to find a few worth applying to
- "Entry-level" listings often aren't really entry level

### What research says
Every source below was opened and checked.

**"Entry-level" jobs that ask for experience**
- 27.3% of 11,920 entry-level-titled postings require at least 1 year of experience. 6.5% require 3+ years. US rate for 3+ years: 11.8%.
  - Source: Hirebase, "The Experience Inflation Index," Aug 19, 2026. https://www.hirebase.org/blogs/entry-level-jobs-experience-inflation
  - Caveat: Hirebase sells job data, so this is a vendor analysis.
- "35% of LinkedIn job postings required three or more years of experience." (No sample size given.)
  - Source: Allwork.Space, "Experience Inflation," Mar 22, 2022. https://allwork.space/2022/03/experience-inflation-why-a-growing-number-of-entry-level-job-seekers-are-getting-turned-down
- Entry-level seekers *estimate* about 55% of entry-level listings ask for 3+ years. But 93% of hiring managers say they typically require fewer than 3 years. (Skynova survey of 1,000+ seekers and 500 hiring specialists. This is what people believe, not a count of postings.)
  - Source: same Allwork.Space article.

**Business-specific: "entry-level" finance and marketing jobs**
- Finance: 46% of entry-level finance postings that state an experience requirement ask for 3+ years (97 postings). Marketing: 49% (70 postings). Only ~37% of postings state a requirement at all. Small samples, so treat as directional.
  - Source: SeekerScore, July 13, 2026 (1,835 entry-level-titled postings). https://www.seekerscore.com/insights/entry-level-jobs-experience-2026
- Counterpoint: the Bureau of Labor Statistics lists financial analysts, accountants, market research analysts and HR specialists as jobs that typically need *no* prior experience. That suggests postings ask for more than the job usually requires.
  - Source: BLS Career Outlook, Jan 2025. https://www.bls.gov/careeroutlook/2025/article/business-career-options.htm

**Job-site filters don't work well**
- "Irrelevant search results and poor filters were top issues reported by participants on all four job searching websites" (Glassdoor, Indeed, Monster, ZipRecruiter; 213 participants).
  - Source: MeasuringU, Sept 18, 2019. https://measuringu.com/?p=554
- 71.6% called online job search technology frustrating; only 7.9% found it intuitive. (Sample size not given.)
  - Source: Training magazine citing a randrr survey, July 22, 2016. https://trainingmag.com/does-online-job-search-technology-work-for-your-company/

**Ending up in the wrong job**
- About 25% of accounting majors are underemployed (in jobs that don't need a degree). Data year not stated.
  - Source: NY Fed Liberty Street Economics, May 19, 2017. https://libertystreeteconomics.newyorkfed.org/?p=452

**More competition, fewer postings**
- Handshake postings fell more than 16% from Aug 2024 to Aug 2025, while average applications per role rose 26%.
  - Source: Fortune, Nov 19, 2025, citing Handshake. https://fortune.com/2025/11/19/next-level-leadership-gen-z-college-graduates-tough-market-how-to-stand-out-according-to-jobs-experts/

**Time spent searching (this one cuts against problem.md)**
- 71% of students spent time on career research and applications. The majority spent less than 2 hours a week; 24% spent less than 1 hour. (1,000 US four-year students, surveyed Apr–May 2022.)
  - Source: Forage, Apr 10, 2023. https://www.theforage.com/blog/news/forage-career-readiness-survey
  - This doesn't support "5–7 hours a week." It covers all students, not just seniors actively searching. The survey should test the 5–7 hour number directly.
